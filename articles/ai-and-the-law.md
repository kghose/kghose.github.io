(I purposely chose the word "law" over "morality". The law is a codification of
our current, local, majority morality, and is a more precise word to frame a
discussion around.)

After thinking on it a bit the relation between AI and the law is fairly simple.
It's just that this round of AI got out of the gate so fast society hasn't had
time to put on pants and run after it.


## Purely software systems

This is most of where we are now. AI generated code or actions hack into other
services, spam people, write articles on line, masquerade as humans etc.

### Ideally

The law catches up. The AI is (legally) just another program/script you
wrote/employed.

If you, whatever you are, set up the initial system, you are responsible.
Whether you are criminally responsible, civilly responsible, acted out of malice
or out of negligence is a court matter as it currently is for humans and
software. 

### In practice

No doubt there will be attempts to weasel out, but that should only swing the
needle between negligence and malice

Cash flush AI companies may lobby our dear leaders to make excepts for "AI".
With a bit of luck and focus we can push back and say that existing laws are
enough. AI is just another program people are hitting "enter" and setting off.


## Self driving vehicles

(Personal note: Can't wait for safer streets with machines highly trained in
defensive driving and respecting pedestrians and cyclists.)

### Ideally

An accident is the vehicle's fault. The manufacturer is liable. Everyone in the
vehicle is a passenger and the only way they become liable is if they actually
did something like open a door or override the self-drive.

Likely that people stop carrying car insurance, though personal liability will
still exist, same as if you were in an accident not involving driving a vehicle.

This puts the incentive on vehicle manufacturers to get things right, though it
also means more surveillance as occupants might now be recorded if vehicle
manufacturers claim they need this for fault determination.

If we are sensible we can strike a good privacy balance (data stays on the
car/third party data broker, only accessible through court order etc.)

### Practically

Car insurance providers lobby to force owners to still carry insurance, though
market forces will drive down insurance rates as vehicle manufacturers compete
on safety and eventually the individual car insurance market disappears or is
covered by a manufacturer's liability.

I think this one will go in the right direction ...

## Weapons

This will be in as sad a situation as always. As we have seen accountability for
civilian deaths through accidental or purposeful attacks, as well as violations
of other aspects of the Geneva convention is largely unenforced. Having AI
control weapons will not change this.

There is a greater fear of equipping AI with weapons that is not legal but
societal. This should be a separate article but I think, with some luck and lots
of focus we can get through this like we seem to have gotten away with not
nuking our planet (so far, so far).
