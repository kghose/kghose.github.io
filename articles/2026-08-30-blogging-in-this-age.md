I have kept a blog for a long time. The earliest posts I can find are from July
2007, so 20 years now. My blogging goals have remained the same over these two
decades.

1. Keep a set of notes for myself for problems (mostly software related) that I
   had to solve.
1. Write up information or knowledge I gained to consolidate my understanding of
   it.
1. Organize thoughts (practical or philosophical).
1. Give this back (hence the publicly visible blog) to the world, since most of
   the knowledge I gained came from other people putting up what they learned or
   know freely on the internet.

One of these things has changed considerably these past few years: The first
(and arguably the most trivial) goal - note keeping, and I'm pretty happy about
that.

Many of my posts took the form of "Here are the steps that I had to take to get
WiFi working on Ganbanda Linux 1999 edition on my ASUS XYZ MoBo." Stuff that
took me days to figure out (via experimentation, asking questions on forums,
waiting, getting hostile responses and the occasional kind helpful ones (bless
you) and sheer luck) but that often had very short shelf life because everything
changed in six months.

Now, if I go onto Google search and ask questions like "What is the canonical
way to iterate over key-value pairs in a C++ map" or "Why is cmake failing on my
Project Chronos code?" I will get a polite, succinct answer with example code.

I like this much better. As I've said before, this is the [correct culmination
of search](/generative-ai/search): our own diligent and blazingly fast research
librarian.

This means that I've stopped making "How to do X" posts. They were always
ephemeral, and now they are unnecessary, leaving me with the remaining, more
worthy goals of learning things and writing them up to improve my own
understanding and perhaps yours. (And also the occasional rant and rave and bits
of philosophy).
